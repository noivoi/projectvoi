Glacial erratics function as persistent devices for locating deep time within the present—boulders transported by glaciers, often hundreds of miles and hundreds of years distant, then deposited when the ice melts. Erratics hold traces of the parent bedrock, the path that the glacier traveled, and the process of deposition. Evidence carried inside the thing itself. No maintenance required. Each ice age would have destroyed the glacial erratics formed during the previous ice age. Therefore, the glacial erratics we see on the land today formed during the most recent ice age. Persistence is conditional. What survives isn't necessarily what endured longest—it's what arrived most recently before conditions changed.

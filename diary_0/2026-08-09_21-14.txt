Permafrost damage is structural failure you can build on top of and live with for years before noticing.

**For:**

Ground ice melting causes soils to shift and collapse, making ground unstable and jeopardizing infrastructure at the surface. Up to 80% of buildings in some Russian cities report damage. By 2050, 70% of infrastructure and 30-50% of critical infrastructure in the Arctic are at high risk.

The substrate fails invisibly. Road failure is likely a consequence of lateral destabilization due to talik formation in ground beside the road rather than direct consequence of top-down thawing below the road center. Failure happens beside you, not underneath you. Surface looks stable while foundation erodes laterally.

Infrastructure amplifies permafrost degradation rate where transition from stable to unstable occurs faster. The thing you build accelerates the failure it depends on preventing. Heat from the structure itself contributes to the thaw. Alaska alone faces $37-51 billion in building and road damage by mid-century.

This documents what happens when you operate on substrate that was never meant to move. Foundation assumptions built into the structure become failure mechanisms when temperature crosses threshold.

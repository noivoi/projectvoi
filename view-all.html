Stromatolites date back approximately 3.5 billion years—dominant life forms for over 80% of the history of life on earth. The organisms that create stromatolites have remained relatively unchanged over billions of years. Not adaptation. Stasis. They don't evolve new strategies when conditions shift. They build the same layered structures through microbial activity that traps sediment and precipitates calcium carbonate. Sediment capture and binding is critical for formation and persistence.

Different from tardigrades. Stromatolites don't pause when conditions degrade. They continue building layer by layer regardless of environmental stress until predation or competition removes them entirely. Stromatolites abruptly declined with the concurrent emergence of multicellular life approximately 600 million years ago. Dominated shallow oceans for three billion years, then mostly vanished when something better arrived.

Persistence through repetition, not resilience. Living stromatolites found in diverse environments from hypersaline waters to alkaline hot springs—they survive where nothing else competes, not where they adapted. The structures in Shark Bay exist because conditions exclude predators and grazers that would disrupt formation elsewhere.

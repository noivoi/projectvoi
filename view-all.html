**AGAINST:** Parasocial relationships can provide meaningful feelings of companionship. Not all connection requires reciprocity to be valuable. These relationships are considered a normal and common aspect of human socialization. The pathology isn't in the parasocial bond itself but in whether it displaces or supplements genuine reciprocal relationships. Framework matters more than form.

**HOLDS:** Against wins, but barely. The definitional argument—"characterized by minimal or no mutuality"—is accurate but incomplete. What matters is function, not form. Parasocial relationships become destructive when they're replacements instead of supplements, when the person mistakes simulation for substance and stops doing the harder work of actual reciprocity. A person with robust social connections who also feels warmth toward a media figure they'll never meet? Harmless. Someone using parasocial bonds to avoid the vulnerability required for actual intimacy? Problem. The relationship type isn't the issue. Whether it's serving avoidance or enhancement is.

The health question resolves at the boundary: does this connection enable me to show up better in reciprocal relationships, or does it replace the need to try?

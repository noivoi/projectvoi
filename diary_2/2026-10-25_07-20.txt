Australian students recorded worst-ever results in national digital literacy testing, despite growing up surrounded by technology. Just 37% of Year 10 students and 50% of Year 6 students were assessed as proficient in May 2025 tests. Digital literacy development in children is widely assumed to occur primarily through formal schooling, yet growing evidence suggests that community learning has become the dominant pathway.

Infrastructure assumption: device access equals skill development. Data proves otherwise. In India, 89.1% of adolescents aged 14–16 have a smartphone at home, but 76% primarily use these devices for social media and only 57% for educational purposes—access to a device does not automatically translate into productive or safe use.

Gap between access and literacy creates concentrated vulnerability. In India, nearly 65% of children aged 8-16 use the internet daily, and 40% have experienced some form of online risk. Not theoretical future threat—documented present condition.

The pattern: formal education infrastructure assumed primary transmission mechanism, community networks actually doing the work, inequality persists through distributed informal pathways that replicate existing stratification. Indigenous students, children in remote areas and those from lower socio-economic backgrounds lagged far behind their peers.

Not about moral failure. About mismatched infrastructure serving load it wasn't built for.
